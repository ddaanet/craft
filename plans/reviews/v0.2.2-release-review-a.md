## Findings

### 1. SKILL.md deletes a "retired" decision; maintaining-entries says not to delete a decision whose component died

- Proposed severity: **medium**.
- Where: `skills/design-doc-writing/SKILL.md:47-49` against
  `skills/design-doc-writing/references/maintaining-entries.md:38-48, 61-67`.
- SKILL.md now says:
  "**A decision that is inverted or retired is deleted from the doc.** The
  commit that deletes it says why, and the diff keeps the text."
  maintaining-entries.md §Rewiring a dead component says that when a component
  is removed, "Neither of the two easy moves is right: leaving the stale
  reference standing, or deleting the decision along with the thing it named",
  "the decision usually outlives its mechanism — deleting it invites
  re-litigating", and for "**Nothing replaced it** → say so inline".
- Failure scenario: an agent removes a component, loads only the hub (nodes are
  opt-in), reads "a decision that is ... retired is deleted", treats the
  decision about the removed component as retired, and deletes it. The node
  prescribes the opposite for exactly that case. "Retired" is also the word the
  hub and node use for ids (§Numbering, §Retiring an entry), so a reader has no
  way to tell "retired decision" from "decision whose component was retired".
- Suggested fix: in SKILL.md, say what qualifies, so the rule stops covering the
  dead-component case, e.g. "A decision that no longer holds — inverted, or
  withdrawn — is deleted ... A decision whose component was removed is rewired,
  not deleted; see [Maintaining entries](references/maintaining-entries.md)." Or
  drop "or retired" and keep "inverted".

### 2. `git log -- <path>` misses history across a rename or split

- Proposed severity: **medium**.
- Where: `skills/design-doc-writing/SKILL.md:31-32`; `docs/design.md:8`.
- The skill now sends a reader to "commit bodies and `git log -- <path>`" for
  how we got here. A path-limited log does not follow renames, and the splitting
  node tells authors to move content into new files. Probe:
  `git log --oneline -- skills/directive-writing/references/comments-and-commits.md`
  returns only `dda3839`, the rename. With `--follow` it returns `8a5e937` as
  well.
- Failure scenario: a reader looking for why a split or renamed node says what
  it says runs the command as written, gets only the last commit, and concludes
  there is no history.
- Suggested fix: write `git log --follow -- <path>` in both places. Do not
  enumerate further cases: content moved by a split is not followed by
  `--follow` either, and a list of those cases would not be closed.

### 3. The reason for vendoring the toolkit was dropped with the changelog

- Proposed severity: **medium**.
- Where: `docs/design.md:77-81` (FR-9) and `:111-115` (NFR-4), against the
  deleted `docs/changelog.md:281-286` at `v0.2.2`.
- The changelog held the only statement of why the toolkit is vendored: "the
  release recipe has to work in a repo that has never been released, which a
  runtime dependency resolved from a marketplace cannot guarantee". The design
  states that it is vendored, not why. The shipped skill
  (`SKILL.md:37-39, 61-63`) says the reason a decision stands stays in the doc,
  and is folded in before its record goes.
- Failure scenario: a session asks why plugin-dev is not taken from the
  marketplace, finds nothing in the design, and re-litigates it.
- Suggested fix: add the reason to FR-9 or NFR-4, or make it a D-entry that
  carries *Rejected: a marketplace dependency*. The fork also named standalone
  rejections from the changelog that were not folded in: the forms-grep for
  removing-cleanly, spec-hygiene's writer-side rule, and three test-discipline
  drops. Check each against `git show v0.2.2:docs/changelog.md` and fold in only
  the ones whose argument is not already in a skill.

### 4. "Rewording returns almost nothing" lost its mechanism under the byte cap

- Proposed severity: **medium**.
- Where:
  `skills/design-doc-writing/references/splitting-an-oversized-doc.md:51-53`.
- The old text explained the claim: a hard-wrapping formatter refills the lines
  that were just emptied, so rewording frees no lines. This range deleted that
  mechanism and kept the conclusion, now backed by a measurement "in lines".
  Under a byte cap every word cut returns its bytes, and the same file's opening
  measures trimming at ~5.6%.
- Failure scenario: an author budgeting against 20000 bytes skips a concision
  pass the text calls worthless and makes a structural cut earlier than needed.
- Suggested fix: drop the claim, as the shared rule says to do for an
  unsupported claim. Keep the "measure after the planned moves rather than
  estimating" sentence and its 96-line instance. Or scope the claim: "under a
  line cap with a reflowing formatter".

### 5. FR-5 and CLAUDE.md say "any other file", but the gate skips `plans/` and `plugin-dev/`

- Proposed severity: **medium**.
- Where: `docs/design.md:45-48`; `CLAUDE.md:6-7`.
- `plugin-dev/check-size.sh` `is_builtin` skips `plans/`, `plugin-dev/` and
  lockfiles, and the scan also skips binaries and symlinks. Its sign-off on HEAD
  reads "skipped: 62 plans/plugin-dev/lockfiles".
- Failure scenario: a maintainer trusts the gate to catch an oversized plan, and
  it passes silently. The requirement says something the gate does not do.
- Suggested fix: FR-5 says "any other file outside `plans/` and the vendored
  `plugin-dev/`". That is a closed list taken from `is_builtin`. Leave lockfiles
  and binaries out, or say "the toolkit's built-in skips". In CLAUDE.md, say
  "any other tracked file `check-size` scans".

### 6. D-8 says spent briefs are kept; the range deleted a filed brief that a kept brief still points at

- Proposed severity: **medium**. This is a policy fork, so triage may route it
  to my human partner.
- Where: `docs/design.md:211-215` (D-8: "deleting a spent one throws that
  away"), against `1721316`, which deleted
  `plans/2026-10-31-brief-size-cap-in-nonws-bytes.md`.
  `plans/2026-10-31-brief-size-cap-in-bytes.md:6` and `:61` still name that file
  and say its touch points "apply as written".
- Failure scenario: a reader following the bytes brief to its touch-point list
  finds no file. D-8 and the repo's own practice prescribe opposite things for a
  superseded brief.
- Suggested fix: either restore the deleted brief, or amend D-8 to say a brief
  wholly superseded by a later one may be deleted once that one is triaged, and
  point the bytes brief at `git show 1721316^:plans/...`. Amending D-8 adds
  surface, so propose it rather than apply it.
